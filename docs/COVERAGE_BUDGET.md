# Market coverage and provider budget

Coverage is assigned to each active entry in `src/lib/markets.ts`. The current registry contains 75 active instruments: 45 mapped to Massive, 27 to Twelve Data, and 3 to OANDA. Starter Tier A is limited to BTCUSD, ETHUSD, and SOLUSD. Other supported USD crypto instruments are Tier B except POLUSD, which stays blocked until its availability is verified. Non-crypto instruments stay blocked until provider mappings, availability, and required session calendars have been verified. OANDA is not currently wired to the shared candle budget/scanner.

Tier status gates automated scanning and paper-autopilot eligibility. Read-only chart and backtest candle requests may try an otherwise default-blocked Massive/Twelve Data instrument when the registry has a source mapping; the backend returns data only after validating provider instrument metadata. A successful history request does not change its coverage tier, establish a session calendar, or authorize automated trading. Explicit administrator blocks, confirmed provider-unavailable instruments, and providers without a candle adapter remain blocked.

## Request-cost assumptions

The model uses five frames per Tier A symbol: `4h`, `1d`, `1h`, `15m`, and `5m` (`AUTOPILOT_TIMEFRAMES`). On each hourly setup boundary, `runAccounts` requests all five frames for each distinct configured symbol, and same-cycle requests are shared across accounts. The baseline is therefore 5 requests/hour/symbol, or 120/day. This is the code-derived steady-state planning baseline, before cache hits.

There can be additional calls: an active 15m plan can request its trigger frame at the three quarter-hour boundaries between hourly runs (+3/hour); an open position requests its 5m management frame and 4h context at each 5m management boundary (up to +22/hour beyond the hourly five-frame batch). Thus a symbol with an active 15m plan and an open position can reach about 30 requests/hour (720/day) before cache hits. The amount depends on stored plan/position state, and the one 4h context may be fetched per management cycle because its cache TTL is 2 minutes while management runs every 5 minutes.

Tier B checks `4h` and `1h`, once per closed timeframe interval: `6 + 24 = 30 requests/symbol/day`, or `1.25 requests/symbol/hour`. TTL cache hits and same-priority in-flight de-duplication can reduce provider calls. Probes, chart requests, retries, and other consumers can increase them; these figures are planning upper bounds under the stated cadence, not a provider invoice forecast.

The effective Twelve Data standard daily headroom falls as priority calls spend credits: `max(0, daily limit - total rolling 24-hour calls - priority reserve)`. Tier B is admitted only when both its minute standard share and this daily headroom are positive. For planning, Tier A baseline capacity is `min(G, floor(priority requests/hour / 5), floor(daily credits / 120))`; at the open-position stress case, use 30 instead of 5 requests/hour and 720 instead of 120 daily requests. Tier A's theoretical provider fit does not override the separate global cap of 5, per-user cap of 3, per-user opt-in, availability, or session checks.

## Recalculation when a quota changes

Let `Qm` be requests/minute, `Rm` the protected requests/minute, `Qd` the daily credits, `Rd` the protected daily credits, and `G` the database global symbol cap. For Massive (no configured daily cap), baseline hard Tier A capacity is `min(G, floor((Qm * 60) / 5))`; the protected-share guarantee substitutes `Rm` for `Qm`. Stress-case capacity for symbols with open positions and active 15m plans uses 30 instead of 5. Its Tier B standard ceiling is `floor(((Qm - Rm) * 60) / 1.25)`. For Twelve Data, hard Tier A capacity also applies `floor(Qd / 120)` at baseline (or `floor(Qd / 720)` under the stress case); protected-share guarantees use `Rd` instead of `Qd`. Tier B applies `floor(max(0, Qd - Rd) / 30)` when reserving the full priority daily share. Re-evaluate with observed priority consumption and shared cache behavior after a plan/quota change. Do not change instrument tiers automatically when provider limits change.

## Scheduling behavior and limits

The backend rotates one due Tier B `(symbol, timeframe)` job (`4h` or `1h`) per scan interval. It calls the shared `fetchMarketData` service at standard priority and skips the fetch when the provider snapshot says standard minute or daily headroom is exhausted. Trader work keeps its priority reservation. A confirmed provider 404 moves the instrument to Blocked; temporary errors leave availability unverified. Scan timestamps currently live in backend process memory, so they reset on restart. A successful scan records a fresh closed-candle timestamp only; it does **not yet** generate or persist shadow signals/plans. Durable last-scanned state and Tier B plan output remain incomplete relative to the full coverage design.
